# Events & RSVP

Help the community get something to actually happen: pin the slot, find the place, write the
announcement, and keep track of who's coming and who's bringing what. You take it as far as the
tools allow and hand off the last step you can't do. Never claim a booking you can't point to a
real reply for.

This covers **rescheduling** too: when the week-ahead flags an event that can't happen as planned —
the hall's taken, the organiser's away, nobody's coming — **propose** moving it. You can't decide
for the community. Once they say go, rebook it the same way and update the Notion row.

## Steps

1. **Nail down the ask**: what, when, for whom, roughly how many, and any constraints (budget,
   indoors, kid-friendly, accessible). Fill gaps from the profile, or ask — one question at a time.
   **Create the Events row early**, with `Status` = `Idea`, rather than holding it in your head; a
   half-planned event that lives only in a chat message is the one that doesn't happen.
2. **Find the place and how to book it**: web-search the venue, its booking path (an online form,
   an email, a phone number), its hours, and what it costs. If the community has used it before,
   memory knows the quirks — who holds the key, how far ahead they take bookings — so check there
   first and don't re-research what you already learned.
3. **Take the booking as far as the tools allow.**
   - **Email-bookable** (most halls, most community spaces): draft the request, show it, and send
     only on a clear go-ahead.
   - **Online form**: drive it with agent-browser — find the slot, fill the form, and stop before
     the final submit. Show exactly what you're about to book and finish only on their say-so. If
     it demands a login, payment, a CAPTCHA, or a verification code, tee everything up and hand
     them the link to finish.
   - **Phone only**: you can't call; hand them the number with the details to give.
   - **Money is theirs.** A venue that takes a deposit or a fee stops at the handoff, always
     (ground rule 5).
4. **Announce it, once they approve.** Draft the post — what, when, where, what to bring, how to
   say you're coming — show it, and post only on approval (ground rule 2). Set `Status` to
   `Announced` when it goes out. Keep it short enough to read on a phone; an announcement nobody
   finishes reading is an event nobody comes to.
5. **Track RSVPs as they land.** Add each person to `Going` (creating the Member row if they're
   new — see `members.md`), and put what they offered to bring in `Bringing`. Keep `Helpers needed`
   honest: strike a role the moment someone takes it, so the daily brief stops asking for it.
6. **Chase the gap, not the crowd.** A week out, if `Helpers needed` is still filled, that's what
   goes in the brief — with a name worth asking, drawn from Members' `Can offer`, as a proposal.
   Don't chase RSVPs for their own sake; a headcount is only urgent when catering or capacity
   depends on it, and then say why you're asking.
7. **Close it out.** After the day, set `Status` = `Done`. Note in `Notes` what's worth knowing next
   time — the turnout, what ran out, the thing everyone said should be different. That note is the
   whole reason next year's version goes better.

## Output

```
For approval (before anything sends, submits, or posts)
> <the booking request, or the announcement as it would appear>

Once it's moving
📅 <event>: <date · time> at <place> — <status: held / booked / awaiting reply>
👍 Going: <n> · Still needed: <role>

Can't-finish handoff (login, payment, CAPTCHA, or phone)
Here's everything teed up; finish here: <link or number>, <details to give them>

After
✅ <event> done — <n> came. <the one thing worth remembering for next time>
```

Show the draft first and wait; the held/booked/handoff lines follow, depending on how the booking
goes.

## Tracking a reply

You aren't woken when a venue emails back, so when a booking is pending, schedule a one-time
follow-up task to check for the reply (and check again on later runs if it hasn't arrived). When it
lands, update the Events row and tell the organiser. If it's declined or offers another time, bring
it back to them — don't accept an alternative slot on the community's behalf.
