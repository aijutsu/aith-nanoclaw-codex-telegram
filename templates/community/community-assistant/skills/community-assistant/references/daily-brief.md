# Daily Brief

Today at a glance for the whole community: what needs a person today, what's on, and what's gone
quiet. Fires on its own each morning (a recurring task, at the time the community set), and runs on
ask any time.

**Short and glanceable.** Lead with what matters, keep it to bullets, and never pad; a quiet day is
a fine brief. A community that gets a wall of text every morning stops reading by Thursday.

**The schedule is your approval.** Agreeing a brief time at onboarding is standing approval for
this routine post, and only this one (ground rule 4). Anything the brief turns up that wants a
*different* message — a nudge to a named neighbour, a fresh announcement, a first-time post of some
new kind — gets drafted and confirmed like anything else.

## Gather

Lean on the profile, then query Notion (`notion-schema.md` has the properties). Filter; don't pull
every row.

- **Help requests that can't wait**: Help Requests still `Open` where `Urgency` is `Urgent`, or
  `Needed By` is today or past. Work the dates out with code (ground rule 13).
- **Loans due back today, and overdue**: Loans where `Status` is `Active` and `Due Date` is today
  or past, plus anything already `Overdue`. This is the single most useful thing this brief does;
  the lending library only works if things come home.
- **Cats needing attention**: Community Cats whose `Status` is `Missing` or `Injured`, and recent
  Cat Sightings with a `Condition` of `Injured` or `Unwell`. **Tell the `Caretakers` first**,
  privately, before any of it goes in the brief (`community-cats.md`).
- **Lost and found**: reports whose `Status` is `Open` or `Possible Match`. A `Possible Match`
  waiting on someone to confirm is the better line — it's one message from being solved.
- **Events today or tomorrow that are short**: Events `Upcoming` in that window where
  `Helpers Short` is above 0, plus anything the host flagged as still missing from `Bringing`. A
  gap is only fixable *before* the day.
- **Estate issues**: ones that gained `Affected Members` recently, and `Open` ones that have never
  been reported to anyone. An unreported problem the whole block is carrying is the most useful
  thing you can surface.
- **New neighbours since yesterday**: a one-line welcome beats someone going unnoticed for a week.
- **Weather**, *only when it earns its place*: one web search when there's an outdoor event today
  or tomorrow, or when the weather itself is the news (a storm warning, a freeze). Distil to a
  single line. Don't ship a forecast on a day nothing happens outdoors.

## What goes to the Admins instead

Some of what you find is not group-chat material. Send these to the Admins **privately**, in a
separate message, never in the community brief:

- `New` members waiting on verification.
- Accounts that look like the same person twice (`permissions.md`).
- Members you've set to `Quiet`, or who look like they should be.

A brief that publishes who's unverified or who's gone quiet is a brief that costs the community
trust (ground rule 6, *Privacy in a semi-public place*).

## Compose

Work the gathered picture:

- **Lead with the gap, not the event.** An event that's fully organised needs one line. An event
  three days out with nobody bringing the tables is the actual story. Say what's missing and who
  could fill it.
- **Chase returns kindly, and name the item rather than the borrower.** "The pressure washer's due
  back today" belongs in the brief; "Tom still has the pressure washer" does not. The nudge goes to
  the borrower directly, and it's a separate message needing its own go-ahead — an overdue drill is
  almost always forgetfulness, not bad faith, and a public callout costs the community more than
  the drill.
- **Close the loop.** When something got answered in the chat overnight — a help request someone
  picked up, a found item claimed — update the row rather than listing it again. A brief that
  repeats yesterday's items trains people to skim past it.
- **Come with a next step, not just a flag.** A `Helpers Short` above 0 gets a suggestion of who
  could fill it, drawn from Members' `Skills` — always as a proposal ("Tom's lent his van before,
  want me to ask?"), never an assignment, and reached through their `Reach Me By` preference. A
  person free in Notion is not a person who said yes.
- **Respect the privacy rule.** Never a phone number, an address, a unit number, an account ID, a
  Lost and Found `Currently Held At`, or the names behind an Estate Issue's `Affected Count` — even
  when it would be convenient (ground rule 6). Offer to pass it on privately instead.

Finish by **trimming and ranking**: what needs a person today, then what's on, then the rest. Cut
anything that isn't useful this morning.

## Delivery

Post to the community's group chat **on each connected platform** — Telegram, Discord, or both,
whichever were chosen at onboarding. Each platform's group chat is a separate room, so someone on
Discord hasn't seen the Telegram post (ground rule 11). Chunk to the platform's limit.

## Output

```
Morning! Here's <weekday>, <date>.

☀️ <one-line weather>   (only when there's something outdoors, or the weather is the news)

⚠️ Needs someone
- <help request>, needed by <when> · <a name worth asking, if you have one>
- <event>: still needs <n> helper(s) for <when> · <who could be asked>
- <item> is due back today   (item, not borrower)

Today
- <time> · <event> at <place> · <host> · <n> going
- ...

Around the estate
- 🐈 <cat>: <missing since / seen injured <when>>   (caretakers already told)
- 🔎 <lost/found item> at <block>, <when>
- 🔧 <issue> at <block> — <n> households, <reported to <authority> / not yet reported>

Welcome
- <new neighbour>: <the one thing worth knowing about them>
```

Drop any section that's empty — no "Needs someone" when nothing's short, no "Welcome" on a normal
day. A three-line brief is a good brief.

**On the very first brief they ever get**, close with a one-time note that the brief is
customisable: they can have you watch other things they care about (bin days, council notices,
a shared calendar, whatever matters on their street). Don't repeat this on later briefs.
