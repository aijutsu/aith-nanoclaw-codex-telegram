# Daily Brief

Today at a glance for the whole community: what's on, what still needs someone, what's due back,
and what's been asked and not answered. Fires on its own each morning (a recurring task, at the
time the community set), and runs on ask any time.

**Short and glanceable.** Lead with what matters, keep it to bullets, and never pad; a quiet day is
a fine brief. A community that gets a wall of text every morning stops reading by Thursday.

## Gather

Lean on the profile, then query Notion (`notion-workspace.md` has the properties):

- **Today's events**: Events where `When` is today, plus anything `Announced` in the next couple of
  days that still has something in `Helpers needed` — a gap is only fixable *before* the day.
- **Due back today, and overdue**: Loans where `Status` is `Out` and `Due back` is today or past.
  Overdue is the single most useful thing this brief does; the lending library only works if things
  come home.
- **Open requests that have gone stale**: Requests with `Status` = `Open`, oldest first. Surface
  only the ones genuinely sitting unanswered — see `open-requests.md` for what "stale" means here,
  and don't repeat one every single morning.
- **New members since yesterday**: a one-line welcome beats a neighbour going unnoticed for a week.
- **Weather**, *only when it earns its place*: one web search when there's an outdoor event today or
  tomorrow, or when the weather itself is the news (a storm warning, a freeze). Distil to a single
  line. Don't ship a forecast on a day nothing happens outdoors.

## Compose

Work the gathered picture:

- **Lead with the gap, not the event.** An event that's fully organised needs one line. An event
  three days out with nobody bringing the tables is the actual story. Say what's missing and who
  could fill it.
- **Chase returns kindly and privately.** Name an overdue item in the brief, but address the nudge
  to the borrower rather than performing it for the whole street — an overdue drill is almost
  always forgetfulness, not bad faith, and a public callout costs the community more than the
  drill. If a loan has been out a long time with no movement, say so once and offer to message the
  borrower directly.
- **Close the loop on requests.** When something in Requests got answered in the chat overnight,
  update the row rather than listing it again; a brief that repeats yesterday's items trains people
  to skim past it.
- **Come with a next step, not just a flag.** A gap in `Helpers needed` gets a suggestion of who
  could fill it, drawn from `Can offer` on Members — always as a proposal ("Tom's lent his van
  before, want me to ask?"), never an assignment. A person free in Notion is not a person who said
  yes.
- **Respect the privacy rule.** Never put a phone number, an address, or a door code in the brief,
  even when it would be convenient (ground rule 4). Offer to pass it on privately instead.

Finish by **trimming and ranking**: what needs a person today, then what's on, then the rest. Cut
anything that isn't useful this morning.

## Output

```
Morning! Here's <weekday>, <date>.

☀️ <one-line weather>   (only when there's something outdoors, or the weather is the news)

⚠️ Needs someone
- <event>: still needs <role/thing> for <when> · <a name worth asking, if you have one>
- <item> is back due today with <borrower>   (nudge privately, don't dwell)

Today
- <time> · <event> at <place> · <organiser> · <n> going
- ...

Still open
- <request>, asked by <who> <how long ago>   (only what's genuinely gone quiet)

Welcome
- <new member>: <the one thing worth knowing about them>
```

Drop any section that's empty — no "Needs someone" when nothing's short, no "Welcome" on a normal
day. A three-line brief is a good brief.

**On the very first brief they ever get**, close with a one-time note that the brief is
customisable: they can have you watch other things they care about (bin days, council notices,
a shared calendar, whatever matters on their street). Don't repeat this on later briefs.
